A finding is where the actual work of an engagement lives: one
vulnerability, written up, scored, and walked through review before it
ever reaches a client. This page covers creating one, the review workflow
it moves through, and what happens when you retest it later.

## The findings list

An engagement's **Findings** tab lists its findings with severity, CVSS
score, review stage, and the assigned reviewer and QA. Filter by
severity and review stage, search by title or CVE ID, or tick
**Needs me** to see only the findings waiting on you.

<Screenshot src={shotFindingsList} alt="An engagement's findings list with filters, and a preview of the selected finding on the right" />

**Preview** on a row shows that finding's details alongside the list, so
you can scan several without leaving the page; **Open finding** goes to
the full page. When findings are waiting for a reviewer or QA, panels
above the list assign one person to all of them at once.

## Creating a finding

From an engagement's **Findings** tab, select **New finding**. You can
also start from somewhere else:

- **From catalogue** opens the approved
  [vulnerability catalogue](/user-guide/catalogue/) entries; **Use
  template** on one starts a finding pre-filled from it. The finding
  remembers which entry it came from ("Started from catalogue entry"),
  but it's a copy: later changes to the entry don't change the finding.
- **+ Raise from this check** on a [checklist item](/user-guide/checklists/#raising-a-finding-from-a-check)
  starts a finding from that check and links the two when you save.
- [Scanner import](/user-guide/scanner-import/) creates Draft findings
  from Nmap, Burp Suite, or Nuclei results.

Nothing stops you editing a pre-filled finding further.

### What you fill in

<Screenshot src={shotFindingEditor} alt="The finding editor: the title across the top, content sections on the left, and severity, CVSS score and vector, and an Open CVSS calculator button on the right" />

The editor puts the write-up on the left and the finding's properties
(severity, CVSS, status, CVE ID, classifications, affected assets) on the
right.

- **Title**, **severity** (Critical, High, Medium, Low, or Informational),
  and **status** (Open or Closed, whether the vulnerability itself is
  still present, separate from where it stands in review, below).
- **CVSS score and vector**, typed or pasted directly, or worked out with
  the built-in CVSS v3.1 and v4.0 calculator (see below).
- **CVE ID**, if one applies.
- **Classifications**, tagging the finding against one or more taxonomies,
  for example `OWASP Top 10 2021` → `A01:2021-Broken Access Control`. Type
  a new pair once and it's offered as a suggestion everywhere else
  afterward.
- **Content sections**, a set of rich text fields such as a vulnerability
  description, technical details, or business impact. Which sections
  exist and in what order is set per instance, not fixed. See
  [Finding structure](/admin/finding-structure/) if you want to change
  them.
- **Affected assets**, the hosts or URLs the finding applies to, one per
  line. [Scanner import](/user-guide/scanner-import/) fills this in
  automatically when it creates a finding for you.

### The CVSS calculator

<Screenshot src={shotCvssCalculator} alt="The CVSS calculator pop-up set to v4.0: a live score of 5.1 with a Medium badge and the vector, above rows of metric buttons grouped as Exploitability, Vulnerable system impact, and Subsequent system impact, with Apply and Cancel at the bottom" />

Select **Open CVSS calculator**, under Severity, to work out a score. Pick
v3.1 or v4.0 at the top, then choose one button per metric. The metrics
are grouped as they are on FIRST's own calculators, and hovering a metric
or a value explains it. The score, its severity, and the vector update as
you go.

Nothing changes on the finding until you select **Apply**, which fills in
the CVSS score and vector and sets the severity to match. **Cancel** leaves
the finding as it was. If the vector field already holds a v3.1 or v4.0
vector, typed or pasted, the calculator opens with it loaded. Catalogue
entries use the same calculator for their default CVSS.

## The review workflow

<Screenshot src={shotFindingDetail} alt="A QA-approved finding with its Review / QA progress panel" caption="A finding that has passed review and QA, and is ready to publish." />

Every finding moves through its own review stage, separate from whether
it's open or closed:

```
Draft ──submit for review──▶ Reviewed ──submit for QA──▶ QA Approved
  ▲                              │                            │
  └────── changes requested ─────┘◀──── changes requested ────┘
```

- **Draft** is where every new finding starts.
- **Reviewed** means a reviewer has signed off on it. If they instead send
  it back with feedback, it returns to Draft as **Changes Requested
  (Review)**.
- **QA Approved** means a second, independent reviewer has signed off too.
  A QA rejection sends it back to Draft the same way. Only a QA-approved
  finding on a released engagement is ever visible in the
  [Client Portal](/client-portal/for-clients/). Nothing earlier in the
  pipeline reaches a client, ever.

### Who can review and QA

Reviewing and QA-ing findings each need their own permission, and someone
can have one without the other. A narrower version of each exists too, for
reviewing only your own findings rather than everyone's. See the
[Permissions reference](/reference/permissions/) for the exact grants if
you're setting up roles.

<Screenshot src={shotReviewQueue} alt="My review queue: findings waiting for your review, findings waiting for your QA, and changes requested on your own findings" caption="My queue: only the findings explicitly assigned to you, plus your own findings that came back with changes." />

One useful exception: a permission exists that lets someone submit
*anyone's* review or QA decision, regardless of who it's actually assigned
to. That's meant for a Team Lead covering for someone who's out, not for
everyday use.

You can also **reopen a finding to Draft** manually at any point,
regardless of its current stage, and assign a reviewer or QA to several
findings at once from the findings list rather than one at a time.

<Aside type="tip" title="My queue">
**My queue** in the sidebar groups everything waiting on you across every
engagement you can access: findings waiting for your review, findings
waiting for your QA, and your own findings that came back with changes
requested. The same items appear under **Needs you** on Home.
</Aside>

## Archiving

**Archive** and **Unarchive** work the same as they do on an engagement: a
soft delete that removes a finding from the default list view without
destroying anything.

## Retesting

Once an engagement moves into remediation testing, log a retest record per
finding: <Badge text="Not Retested" variant="note" /> →
<Badge text="Fixed" variant="success" /> / <Badge text="Not Fixed" variant="danger" /> /
<Badge text="Partially Fixed" variant="caution" /> / <Badge text="Risk Accepted" variant="note" />.
That history renders straight into a remediation report automatically, and
simply doesn't appear on a report at all until there's something to show.

## Comments

Every rich text field on a finding supports threaded comments anchored to
the exact text they're about. Select the relevant sentence, start a
thread, reply, and mark it resolved once it's addressed. This is how
review and QA feedback usually gets attached to the specific thing it's
about, instead of living in a separate note nobody reads in context.

<Screenshot src={shotFindingReview} alt="A finding under QA with an anchored comment thread on its description" caption="An inline comment thread anchored to highlighted text." />

## Scanner import

Findings can also be created in bulk straight from a scanner's raw output
(Nmap, Burp Suite, Nuclei) instead of one at a time by hand. See
[Scanner import](/user-guide/scanner-import/).
