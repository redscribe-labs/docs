Before you install RedScribe, here's what it actually needs to run, and
how to size the machine you put it on.

## What you need

- **Docker and Docker Compose.** The normal way to run RedScribe
  ([Installation Methods 1 through 3](/getting-started/installation/)).
- **Python 3 and a local Postgres**, only if you're working on RedScribe's
  own code without Docker ([Method 4](/getting-started/installation/#method-4-local-development-without-docker)).
- **Node.js and npm**, only if you need to rebuild a frontend bundle after
  changing its source yourself. This isn't needed to run the app day to
  day, and isn't part of the Docker image at all. See
  [Frontend asset builds](/dev/frontend-builds/) if that's you.
- **An existing Tailscale network**, with MagicDNS and HTTPS certificates
  turned on, only for [Method 1](/getting-started/installation/#method-1-docker-compose--tailscale-recommended).

## Sizing

Size RedScribe by how many people are actively using it at the same
moment, not by how many accounts exist in total. Almost every request is
quick, and the one thing that ties up real resources for a while is
generating a report.

| Tier | Active at once · total accounts | vCPU (min → recommended) | RAM (min → recommended) | Disk | Report jobs at once |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | 1 user | 1 | 1 → 2 GB | 10 GB+ | 1 |
| Pilot | up to 5 · up to 10 | 1 → 2 | 2 → 4 GB | 20 GB | 2 |
| Small firm | 10 to 15 · 10 to 30 | 2 → 4 | 4 → 8 GB | 40 to 50 GB | 2 |
| Mid size firm | 25 to 40 · 30 to 80 | 4 → 8 | 8 → 16 GB | 100 GB+ | 3 to 4 |
| Larger firm | 50+ · 80 to 150+ | 8 → 16 | 16 → 32 GB | 200 GB+ | 4 to 6 |

"Report jobs at once" is how many exports (PDF, DOCX, and so on) can run
in the background simultaneously before the next one has to wait its
turn, set from the Report Profiles page. See
[Reports & exports](/user-guide/reports/#export-concurrency) for what
that limit actually protects.

These come from how the app actually behaves under load, not a formal load
test, so keep an eye on how long report exports take on your largest
engagement before committing to a tier. A single person kicking the tires
can get by on even less than the Solo row, a 1 vCPU, 1 GB box, but that's a
floor for trying it out, not somewhere to stay once a second person or a
report export joins in.

<Aside type="caution" title="Past &quot;Larger firm&quot;">
At that scale, stop scaling the box itself. The better move is splitting
the database onto its own host and adding a background job queue, so
report generation stops competing with ordinary browsing for the same
limited resources. Neither of those exists yet as a built-in option, see
[Scaling & concurrency model](/dev/scaling/) for what that change would
involve and why report generation is the first thing to run out of
headroom.
</Aside>

### What shifts these numbers

- **Add RAM before CPU.** Rendering a report spikes hardest on
  image-heavy findings, so if your team pastes in a lot of screenshots,
  bias toward more memory first.
- **A shared host.** These figures assume the app and its database share
  one machine, as shipped. Moving the database to its own host frees up
  headroom on both sides independently.
- **Disk usage grows** with stored evidence and how long you keep backups
  around, not with how much data is in the database schema itself.
- **Engagements don't contend with each other.** At most two people are
  ever actively working the same engagement at once, by design, so total
  account count and how busy the system feels scale independently of each
  other.
