An engagement is the unit of work in RedScribe: its scope, team, checklist, and findings all hang off it. A little discipline here pays off in a clean report at the end.

## Define scope precisely, up front

Scope is structured — typed assets under named [asset groups](/user-guide/engagements/#scope-and-asset-groups) — so use that structure instead of a vague sentence.

- Enter each in-scope target as its own asset with the right type (URL, IP, subnet, FQDN). Use the notes field for caveats ("management interfaces only", "excludes staging").
- For a network test, split scope into the groups you'll report on — typically **Internal** and **External** — from the start, so findings can be tagged as you go rather than retrofitted.
- Keep scope and the engagement's test type honest; they drive the report's framing.

## Change scope through the request, not around it

When scope shifts mid-engagement, use the [scope-change request](/user-guide/engagements/#scope-changes) so there's a record of what was agreed.

<Aside type="note">
Approval records the decision and notifies — it doesn't edit the scope. A manager then applies the agreed change on **Manage scope**. That two-step is deliberate: the approval is your paper trail, the edit is the action.
</Aside>

## Let the lifecycle gate delivery

The [status lifecycle](/user-guide/engagements/#status-lifecycle) exists to stop half-finished work reaching a client.

- Move an engagement forward only when the stage is genuinely done; the buttons confirm before changing anything.
- Don't fight the QA gate. An engagement can't reach Approved or Delivered until every active finding is QA-approved — that block is a feature, not an obstacle. Archive a finding you've deliberately set aside rather than leaving it to hold up delivery.

## Treat checklist coverage as evidence

Run a [checklist](/user-guide/checklists/) against the engagement and keep it current. The coverage view is part of your report's story.

- Record an outcome for every check — tested, raised a finding, or blocked. A blocked check with its reason shows the client a limitation you hit, which is better than a silent gap.
- Raise findings straight from a check so the link between what you tested and what you found is kept.

## Split by asset group when it helps the reader

Splitting a report into Internal and External sections is the right call for most network tests and the wrong call for a single-target web-app test.

- Use a profile that [splits findings by asset group](/admin/report-profiles/#splitting-findings-by-asset-group) only when the engagement genuinely has distinct asset classes a reader will want separated.
- When you do, tag every finding as you write it — grouped export is blocked until all are tagged, and tagging 40 findings at the end is nobody's idea of fun.

## Close the loop on retests

When remediation testing happens, record it. [Retest outcomes](/user-guide/findings/#retesting) close, reopen, or leave each finding automatically, which keeps the remediation report accurate without hand-editing statuses.
