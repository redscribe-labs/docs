The rest of the documentation tells you **how** each feature works. This section is our opinion, as the vendor, on **how to use them well** — the defaults and habits we'd set up for a firm running RedScribe in production.

None of this is enforced by the software. It's guidance drawn from how the product is designed to be used, so you can adopt it wholesale or cherry-pick.

## The short version

If you read nothing else:

- **Terminate TLS in front of RedScribe, and treat the root key like a crown jewel** — without it, encrypted data is unrecoverable. [Deployment & hardening](/best-practices/deployment-and-hardening/).
- **Give people the narrowest role that lets them work, and keep review and QA independent of the author** unless you've consciously decided otherwise. [Teams & permissions](/best-practices/teams-and-permissions/).
- **Let the status lifecycle and QA gate do their job** — nothing reaches a client that hasn't been through review and QA. [Running engagements](/best-practices/running-engagements/).
- **Write findings once, in the catalogue, and reuse them** — consistency across reports is a quality signal clients notice. [Finding quality](/best-practices/finding-quality/).
- **Release to the client portal deliberately, not by default**, and decide your retention policy before you need it. [Reporting & delivery](/best-practices/reporting-and-delivery/).
